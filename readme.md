# Signal Identification: JCCM: Joint Conformer and CNN Models for Overlapping Radio Signal Identification [with MATLAB]

JCCM: Joint Conformer and CNN Model for Overlapping Radio Signal Identification
Abstract: With the advancement of radio technology and its widespread application, the identification of overlapping radio signals has attracted increasing attention. When considering both radio propagation effects and low signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) scenarios, traditional blind signal separation (BSS) methods often perform poorly. This paper proposes a joint Conformer and CNN model (JCCM) to separate and identify unknown overlapping radio signals monitored by nodes. The model utilizes the attention mechanism of Conformer to encode the signal spectrum into feature maps, and then perceives global and local features through a convolutional neural network (CNN), which decodes the feature maps into the proportion of signal components.Furthermore, a signal preprocessing module containing the minimum pooling (MinPool) and average pooling (AvgPool) layers is designed for signal denoising. The JCCM is evaluated from three aspects: signal separation, scale-preserving and main signal classification. The results show that the model has better performance especially under low SNR conditions.
Introduction
Identifying overlapping radio signals is crucial for efficient spectrum management, interference suppression, coexistence of wireless systems, signal recognition, and compliance regulation. By identifying overlapping signals, it is possible to determine and categorize the different types of signals present in a specific environment. Understanding the composition of overlapping signals can provide valuable references for network planning, spectrum monitoring, and radio security.
Blind Signal Separation (BSS) technology aims to separate mixed signals into their original components without prior knowledge of the signal sources. Independent Component Analysis (ICA) and Non-negative Matrix Factorization (NMF) are among the BSS algorithms that can be used to separate overlapping radio signals and extract individual components, thereby achieving signal identification and analysis. However, ICA often struggles to accurately determine the number of signal sources and the appropriate scale of the signals; although NMF demonstrates strong scale preservation capabilities in handling signal recognition and separation tasks, its separation and recognition effectiveness still face challenges under low SNR conditions when dealing with overlapping signals that have experienced complex radio propagation.
The core challenges in this scenario can be summarized as follows: (1) In environments with low SNR, the spectral characteristics of overlapping signals are heavily masked by noise, leading to reduced recognition accuracy; (2) During the propagation of wireless signals, the components of overlapping signals experience different propagation losses, resulting in significant scale differences between the components, increasing the difficulty of scale preservation.
In recent years, the field of modulation recognition (MR) has adopted various integrated machine learning methods for extensive research. With the development of deep learning, Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN) have further improved the performance of MR. Research has also proposed a composite attention mechanism that integrates both time and frequency dimensions to address the challenges of wireless communication environments, but these attention mechanisms are mainly used for single-signal modulation classification.
Given this, this paper addresses the issue of overlapping radio signal identification in scenarios where various wireless signals experience complex propagation and unknown monitoring nodes. It proposes a joint Conformer and CNN model (JCCM) to achieve separation, recognition, and scale preservation of overlapping radio signals. This model employs a coding architecture that combines signal preprocessing units with Conformer to enhance the performance of radio signal identification under low SNR conditions. Experiments on identifying overlapping radio signals and main signal classification validate the superior performance of JCCM.

Method Overview:
1. The system model monitors an area randomly distributed with N unknown radio signal sources (URSs) within the monitoring node (MN), which has no prior information about the signal sources such as their categories, positions, or emission powers but can detect that each signal source's received power exceeds its sensitivity. At time t, the signal received by the monitoring node is the sum of signals from all sources after power decay and channel gain, including Gaussian noise. Short-time Fourier Transform (STFT) is used to convert the time-domain signal into the time-frequency domain, obtaining the Power Spectral Density (PSD), which is then used to separate and identify the sources.
2. The JCCM model structure consists of a signal preprocessing module, a Conformer encoder, a CNN decoder, and a component normalization (CN) module. It adopts an "encoder-decoder" joint architecture combined with multi-head self-attention mechanisms and convolution operations for feature extraction.
• Signal preprocessing module: Stacked with MinPool1D and AvgPool1D layers, it filters additive Gaussian white noise through sliding window operations to reduce noise impact on signals, enhancing consistency in feature representation and providing high-quality input for subsequent encoding and decoding.
Conformer Encoder: Merging the strengths of CNN and Transformer, it consists of three parts: convolution down-sampling, linear projection, and Conformer layers. The Conformer layer is composed of multiple Conformer blocks, each containing two class-specific Magnolia-like architectures with feedforward networks in between, sandwiched by multi-head self-attention (MHSA) and convolutional modules, ultimately outputting the encoded feature maps.
•  
CNN decoder: After expanding the dimension of the feature maps output by the encoder, they are processed through three 3x3 kernels Conv2D layer and three 3x3 kernels MaxPool2D layer. Flattened, they are input into a fully connected layer, which is activated by a Sigmoid function to output the feature values of different category signals.
•  
Component normalization module: Normalizes the feature values output by the decoder to avoid computational errors caused by numerical insignificance, ultimately obtaining the proportion of each signal component.
3. Network parameters and training settings: The model input is a signal PSD sequence with dimensions 1×5000. After preprocessing, the vectors are folded into 50×100 dimensions and input to the encoder. The Conformer layer contains 4 Conformer blocks. Encoder dimension is 32; CNN decoder output dimension is 1×8 (corresponding to 8 classes of signals); The Adam optimizer is used to minimize the Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss function. The training batch size is B.
4. Dataset and Experimental Settings
The experiment utilizes the 8-class real wireless signal dataset (802.11a, 802.11b, 802.11g, TD-LTE, FDD-LTE, WCDMA, CDMA2000, GLONASS) transmitted by Anritsu MG3710A signal generator and received by Rohde&Schwarz FSW26 spectrum analyzer, with all signals set to a central frequency of 2.4GHz to simulate large-scale propagation fading within a range of 50-200m. The dataset is divided into training sets, validation sets, and test sets in a ratio of 9:1:1, and trained using the PyTorch framework and a NVIDIA TESLA P4 GPU.The signal separation effect is evaluated using cosine similarity, and the main signal classification performance is assessed by accuracy. It is compared with various models such as NMF, SVR, Lasso, ResNet18.
Conclusion: This paper proposes a JCCM model that integrates signal preprocessing modules for the separation, scale-preservation, and recognition of overlapping radio signals. The model utilizes the multi-head self-attention mechanism from Conformer for feature extraction and generation of feature maps, and leverages CNN to perceive both global and local features for decoding, achieving simultaneous separation, scale-preservation, and classification of main signals. Experimental results show that JCCM exhibits significant advantages under low signal-to-noise ratio conditions, with superior performance in terms of cosine similarity for signal separation and accuracy in main signal classification compared to the contrastive models; the signal preprocessing module further enhances model performance by reducing noise impact and improving consistency in feature extraction.
